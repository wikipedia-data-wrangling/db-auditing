#+title: Four-specialist audit of wiki20260401
#+date: 2026-10-11

* Panel conclusion
The database is a useful, extensively indexed research resource, but the current
collection of derived tables and caches should not be treated as a coherent,
validated analytical release. The RFPP intent repair is applied and its tested
cases remain correct. That repair does not resolve the older build, temporal,
identity, eligibility and causal-design problems identified below.

This audit used four specialist reviews: software engineering, PostgreSQL,
computational social science and Wikipedia data semantics. Their reports and
reproducible evidence are linked below. Database work was read-only; no data,
constraints, indexes, classifications, samples or estimates were changed.

Highest priorities are to isolate production-writing tests, prevent mixed-version
analysis, repair invalid protection episodes and event-time eligibility, reconcile
the residual relationship migration, and rebuild analytical products as a single
versioned release. Statistical identification and historical policy validity
still require additional evidence after those implementation repairs.

* Scope and evidence standard
Target independently verified: =wiki20260401=, port =5434=, PostgreSQL 18.4,
=SQL_ASCII=, approximately 14 TiB. Inventory covers 13 public relations
(including a partitioned parent and its children), 62 analysis relations,
and seven =wiki_relations= views. These are relation counts, not independent
source datasets.

Evidence consists of source review, catalogs, indexed integrity probes, exact
aggregates on bounded-size protection/request/sample relations, read-only RFPP
regression tests, and official Wikipedia/MediaWiki documentation. Query timeouts
were at most 30 seconds. Individual queries used read-only transactions;
results are not one database-wide point-in-time snapshot. The source reviewed
is the current working tree, which may differ from code used to create frozen
outputs; source hashes and repository identities are recorded in the manifest.

The panel did not scan all 1.25 billion revisions, validate every text byte,
perform a restore drill, induce migration failures, run production-writing tests,
or recompute causal estimates. Catalog row estimates are not exact counts.
“Confirmed” below means the stated code path or measured condition is confirmed;
it does not establish unmeasured damage, bias magnitude, or historical causality.

* Implications for interpreting the data
** Relationships and observation units
Measured protection-to-page gaps comprise 2,414 restriction rows referring to
1,487 absent page IDs. This is a real join gap, not permission to delete rows
or fabricate parents. =public.logging.log_page= has zero populated values;
absence of that identifier is not evidence that all logged pages were absent.
Use source-specific linkage and explicit found/provenance flags.

Do not require creation-protected titles to have existing page rows. Existing
page restrictions and protected titles encode different technical objects.
Their documentation supports separate page-ID and namespace/title relationships:
[[https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Manual:Page_restrictions_table][page_restrictions]] and
[[https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Manual:Protected_titles_table][protected_titles]].

There are 144,032 unresolved log identities in the event population and 28,825
log entries resolved by title. These are log counts, not episode counts. They
must enter sample-flow reporting and historical identity sensitivity analyses.
The current quality-cutoff materialization has zero measured article-ID
mismatches in the social-science check; the SQL42 finding must not be transferred
to it merely because both concern quality measurement.

** Protection, policy and request chronology
Requested intent, response, nearby log action, technical restriction type/level,
and policy exposure are distinct measurements. ECR can be enforced without ECP;
pending changes governs review/visibility rather than the same edit barrier as
semi-protection. Current official documentation supports those distinctions,
but historical policy exposure needs dated versions and applicability evidence:
[[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Protection_policy][Protection policy]] and
[[https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Arbitration_Committee/Procedures#Extended_confirmed_restriction][Arbitration procedures]].

The RFPP fetcher asks the API for revision IDs and timestamps but retains only
content in its archive extraction result. It parses archived final text and
signature timestamps, not a stored request-time revision. The repaired parser
preserves observed amendments, but cannot establish the chronology of all edits.
Retain source revision IDs, immutable source bytes/hash and collection time;
validate initial-intent claims against historical revisions where necessary.

** Statistical interpretation
Old analytical products are historical runs using old definitions. They are not
automatically estimates for the repaired population. Unknown intent is a
measurement state, not proof of a different request, and should not be filled
from the later administrator decision.

Granted/denied is not equivalent to protected/unprotected. In the audited
2016–2024 mainspace increase population, 4,436/20,624 denied requests have an
opening event within 30 days; matching remains heuristic. Define whether the
estimand concerns decisions or time-varying exposure, and avoid selecting away
crossovers based on later outcomes.

Causal assumptions—administrator assignment, exclusion, continuity at expiry,
network interference and overlap—are not validated by successful SQL or strong
first-stage prediction. Existing placeholder labels are a positive safeguard.
No direction or numerical magnitude of causal bias is asserted by this audit.

* Remediation sequence and acceptance criteria
1. *Prevent avoidable damage and inconsistent consumption.* Make production
   inaccessible to loader tests; require disposable-database identity checks.
   Block reads of incompatible/invalidated analytical generations. Acceptance:
   refusal tests run in isolation and incompatible-generation fixtures fail.
2. *Repair foundational temporal and identity logic.* Preserve and quarantine
   the 133 invalid episode records for raw-log/precision review, enforce one
   explicit episode as-of rule, unify historical identity and rights lookups,
   and separate restriction type from tier. Acceptance: provenance-backed
   fixtures for moves/recreations, past/future rights, revocations, simultaneous
   restrictions, expired-at-log-time parameters, and cutoff boundaries.
3. *Reconcile relational and operational state.* Record both migration failures
   and an explicit decision for the residual NOT VALID FK; retain unmatched
   source evidence. Establish per-volume storage limits before large writes.
   Acceptance: live catalog matches intended constraint state, migration can
   resume, and a capacity plan covers the actual target volume.
4. *Publish a compatible analytical generation.* Preserve the previous run;
   rebuild eligibility, requested-tier duration medians, sampled requests and
   controls, panels/outcomes, caches, then estimates. Make refresh transactional
   or staged with validated publication. Acceptance: source/code/parser hashes
   match across all products; no stale sampled classifications; old/new membership
   and output comparisons are filed; invalidations clear only after validation.
5. *Validate measurement and research design.* Independently annotate a stratified
   request sample, report missingness/selection by era and outcome, materialize
   pre-decision balance covariates, and assess support, horizon comparability,
   crossover and dependence. Acceptance: diagnostics and failed assumptions are
   retained; conclusions are tied to a declared estimand and supported population.
