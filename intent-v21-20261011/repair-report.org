#+title: RFPP intent v2.1 repair — classification prerequisites
#+date: 2026-10-11

Status: applied; active source/tests and direct cache updated; database verification and rollback rehearsal passed.

* Scope and result

The user explicitly authorized repairing the intent defects blocking request
classification. This narrowly supersedes the earlier deferral of panel fixes for
this repair only. No issue comments, issue-state changes, or other panel fixes
are included.

Candidate version: rfpp-intent-v2.1.0. Population: 217,554 requests.
The final stage changes 1,561 records beyond the version marker: 565 actions and
818 tiers change, with overlap. These are changed classifications/metadata, not
an estimated number of previously erroneous requests.

The implementation corrects:
- Direction words in rationale: requesting semi to reduce disruption remains a
  positive request, not a protection decrease.
- Removal objects: remove semi/ECP/full identifies the origin and targets none.
  Directional reductions to a stated lower tier retain that tier instead.
- Nested bold extraction: the first nested heading no longer consumes subsequent
  body text or a signature; immediately following alternative target spans remain
  part of the request's evidence.
- Explicit body transitions, standalone unprotection, requested-action headings,
  content-edit requests, and extended confirm as an inflection variant of ECP.
- Duration/conditional negation and rationale credentials no longer suppress a
  clear requested tier or add a spurious semi target.
- Response-block and signature boundaries prevent administrative protection
  labels from being treated as requester labels in the covered formats.

Ambiguous compound operations, removal of non-edit restrictions, unknown operation
alternatives, and open tier alternatives remain flagged. They must be excluded or
adjudicated when building a scoped edit-protection classifier. This repair does not
claim comprehensive natural-language interpretation or population accuracy.

* Validation

Nine unittest methods pass, including all 25 saved diagnostic fixtures and targeted
guards for purpose clauses, removals, multiple bold spans, response contamination,
compound operations and duration alternatives. The original removal-of-unnecessary-
protection expectation was intentionally corrected from unspecified target to none.
Other original regression expectations are retained. No example IDs are hard-coded
in the parser; live IDs appear only in tests and audit queries.

The full population was staged from an immutable predecessor snapshot. A deterministic
two-per-change-stratum sample was inspected during refinement; the final exported
sample has 167 rows. This is unblinded diagnostic review, not independent human
validation. The original 83-row review and blank human sheet remain in the draft
workspace's sibling rfpp-intent-validation-20261011 directory.

Population review caught and removed candidate regressions involving broad mixed-
action detection, user links preceding headings, alternatives outside the first bold
span, and duration phrases such as month or more. Full staging was rerun after
substantial refinements. Two final branch-only refinements were applied to all rows
that could enter those branches: open_target_alternative, then evidence containing
or. Their SQL and row counts are preserved. The final function signature is stored
in the stage/run record and checked again before migration.

An initial migration guard rejected hashes serialized under different time zones.
Complete live/backup comparison showed zero changed rows. Hashes were standardized
to UTC after asserting complete row equality. Stage, application, verification and
rollback now use UTC consistently; the guard was not bypassed.

The SQL migration defaults to a complete rehearsal ending in ROLLBACK. It rejects
changed source rows or parser definitions, asserts stage/output agreement, and
compares all non-whitelisted fields against the preserved snapshot. Only the 565
changed-action IDs are rematched using the shared event-matching implementation.
No protection events or raw request/decision/outcome fields are rewritten.

The guarded rollback is rehearsed after application. It checks every post-repair
row hash, restores only derived fields, asserts exact full-row equality with the
predecessor, restores the prior entrypoint definition, and ends with ROLLBACK.
